## UX/UI Patterns

- Top nav segmented by buyer journey stage: Products (Talent Agents, Talent Intelligence suite), Solutions (Enterprise, Public Sector, App Marketplace), Services, Learn, Customers, Events, Company. The "Learn" and "Events" surfaces signal a content-marketing-heavy GTM.
- Page rhythm: hero -> feature demo video carousel -> partnership showcase -> product solutions grid -> core capabilities cards -> customer testimonials -> trust indicators (analyst ratings) -> CTA footer.
- Hero leads with a provocative numeric question ("1 million interviews in 1 hour") instead of a product label — curiosity-driven rather than category-driven entry.
- 25+ enterprise customer logos used as both social proof and as an implicit filter ("we sell to companies like yours").
- Trust indicators (G2, Gartner-style ratings) appear above the final CTA — last-mile reassurance before the demo ask.
- Illustrated character figures recur through the page, giving a consistent visual language across otherwise dense enterprise content.

## Visual Design

- Color palette: navy/dark blue primary, white backgrounds, accent colors reserved for CTAs. Enterprise-trustworthy palette — close cousin to Hire-Bee's dark purple but biased to blue.
- Typography: large, bold sans-serif headlines; tight hierarchical sizing for subheads; clean modern font family. The H1 in the hero is genuinely oversized — a hero "moment," not just a label.
- Imagery: illustrated humans in various poses (not photography) keep the brand light and consistent across cultures/industries; product UI screenshots and video thumbnails handle the "show the product" job.
- Hero composition: oversized question-style headline, dense subhead explaining the mechanism, single dominant CTA with a forward arrow.
- Density: airy at the hero, then dense capability grids below — buyers who need detail get it without sacrificing the marketing wow.

## Copy & Messaging

- Hero headline: "How do you run 1 million interviews in 1 hour?"
- Subhead: "Recruiters can't interview everyone. AI Interviewer reviews applicants, highlighting those who best match your hiring criteria. Your team makes the final decisions."
- Primary CTA: "Try AI Interviewer now →"
- Value-prop bullets (data flex):
  - 1.6B+ career trajectories tracked
  - 1.6M+ skills indexed
  - 10 years aggregated talent data
  - 50+ bias-mitigation variables
- Tone: forward-thinking, enterprise-confident, human-centric. Repeated motif of "humans and agents, united" — explicitly positions AI as augmentation, with the line "Your team makes the final decisions" defusing automation anxiety.

## Takeaways for Hire-Bee

- Open with a provocative question, not a label. A Hire-Bee version: "How do you screen 10,000 resumes before lunch?" frames the AI value as scale relief and works for both recruiter dashboards and the AI resume parsing feature already shipped.
- Defuse AI anxiety inline. Eightfold's "Your team makes the final decisions" is a one-line trust unlock that costs nothing and addresses the #1 buyer hesitation about agentic hiring. Hire-Bee should bake similar language into its recruiter-facing copy.
- Lead with proof numbers when available. Even pre-scale, Hire-Bee can surface concrete numbers (resumes parsed, interviews scheduled, CVs generated) as soon as they exist. Specific numerals beat adjectives.
- The navy-on-white palette suggests Hire-Bee's #2a2354 dark purple can confidently invert: dark-purple chrome on white backgrounds for marketing pages, reserving the dark canvas for product surfaces. Eightfold's enterprise feel comes from disciplined accent-color restraint — apply the same restraint to Hire-Bee's purple.
