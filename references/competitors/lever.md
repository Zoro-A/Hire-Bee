# Lever

- **Category:** ATS
- **Scraped:** 2026-05-12

## UX/UI Patterns

- Top nav is intentionally narrow: Solutions, Compare, Resources, Company — with two persistent CTAs ("Request Demo", "Pricing") anchored to the right. Keeps cognitive load low for first-time visitors.
- Landing page follows a high-trust pattern: Hero -> Results metrics (3 KPIs) -> Product Overview -> "Why Lever" 4-card feature grid -> Industry trust strip (20+ industries) -> G2 / Hackett Group badges -> Testimonials (4 quotes) -> FAQ -> Footer.
- Heavy use of side-by-side product UI screenshots showing real candidate cards, fit scores, and pipeline timelines instead of abstract illustrations.
- "Why Lever" framed as four parallel feature cards: Efficiency, AI-Powered Insights, Fraud Prevention, Scalability — each card promotes a single concrete benefit rather than a feature list.
- Compare-section in the nav surfaces head-to-head pages vs. competitors (Greenhouse, Workable, etc.), exploiting bottom-of-funnel research traffic and comparison-shopping intent.
- FAQ section appears on the landing page itself (not buried in a help center) to deflect pre-sales objections inline.
- Testimonials quote named recruiters and TA leaders with job titles, not just company logos — humanizing the social proof.
- Industry-trust strip groups verticals (Tech, Healthcare, Finance, etc.) rather than listing brand logos — useful when individual logo permissions are limited.
- Pricing kept as a secondary but always-visible CTA, indicating Lever wants to qualify high-intent self-serve traffic without forcing a demo gate.

## Visual Design

- Color palette: corporate blue primary (logo-driven, roughly #1F4FE8/#3B82F6 family), white / light-gray surfaces, green accents used for verification ticks and positive signals. Minimal saturated color overall.
- Typography: bold sans-serif headlines (likely Inter or a Geist-family typeface), professional sans-serif body. Strong weight contrast between H1 and supporting copy.
- Spacing/density: generous vertical rhythm at the section level, but feature cards pack 4-up on desktop — denser than peers, signaling capability breadth without feeling cluttered.
- Hero composition: video background behind headline + subhead + dual CTAs. Product UI overlays establish credibility immediately.
- Imagery: real product dashboards, fit-score visualizations, candidate avatars, and human-centric testimonial photography. Tech-forward but humanized — never illustration-only.
- Iconography: simple line icons paired with each "Why Lever" card; consistent stroke weight reinforces a minimal, engineering-credible aesthetic.

## Copy & Messaging

- Hero headline: "Hiring That Doesn't Hijack Your Week"
- Subhead: "Every stage of hiring has its own hurdles. Lever clears them, so you can stop living in your inbox and start living your life."
- Primary CTA: "Get a Demo" (secondary: "See Pricing")
- Value-prop bullets: "Faster Time to Hire", "Faster Interview Scheduling", "More Qualified Applicants"
- Tone: Conversational, empathetic, benefit-driven. Uses relatable pain language ("living in your inbox") and pairs it with quantitative proof. Treats the recruiter as the protagonist, not the candidate.
- Voice signature: short declarative sentences, second-person ("you"), and a refusal to use feature names in the hero — outcomes only. Feature names only appear once trust is established further down the page.

## Takeaways for Hire-Bee

- Lead with an empathy-driven recruiter headline rather than a feature list. Hire-Bee's recruiter dashboard CTA on the landing page could swap a generic "Sign up as Recruiter" for outcome copy like "Stop drowning in resumes" — pairs well with the dark-purple #2a2354 ground if a warm accent is added for the hopeful payoff.
- Add a 3-KPI strip below the hero (e.g., "Faster shortlists / Higher-quality applicants / Less screening time") — cheap to ship, raises perceived credibility before the visitor reaches the dashboard screenshots.
- Show actual recruiter-dashboard UI in the marketing hero. Hire-Bee already has a separate recruiter dashboard — surfacing a real screenshot beats abstract illustrations and pre-sells the product to TA buyers.
- Borrow the dual-CTA pattern ("Get started" / "See how it works") instead of role-only signup buttons; role selection can become a second step once intent is captured. This also opens a path to a dedicated comparison page strategy later, mirroring Lever's "Compare" nav slot.
