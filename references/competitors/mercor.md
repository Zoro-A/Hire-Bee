# Mercor

- **URL:** https://mercor.com
- **Category:** AI-native
- **Scraped:** 2026-05-12

## UX/UI Patterns

- Extremely lean top nav: Logo, APEX (with sub-items Agents / SWE / AI Productivity), Research, Enterprise, Experts, Login. Nav doubles as a positioning statement — Mercor is for AI work, period.
- Hero is unusually minimal for an enterprise marketplace: left-aligned headline, supporting copy, two CTAs ("Start working", "Learn more"), then a live grid of 10 featured roles directly under the hero.
- The "Latest roles" grid below the hero is the real conversion engine — visitors see actual paying work ($60–$200/hr) before they even scroll past the fold. Live inventory replaces aspirational copy.
- Page composition: hero -> 10 role cards -> content cards (APEX release, expert stories with real photos) -> compact footer.
- Two-audience structure: experts (job seekers) get the hero CTA; enterprises get a separate top-nav entry. Clean separation of the two sides of the marketplace.
- Mobile hamburger preserves the same menu structure — no dumbed-down mobile nav.

## Visual Design

- Color palette: light backgrounds (white / off-white), dark text, with role-card accent colors providing rhythm down the page. Brand is intentionally quiet — the roles and money are the visual interest.
- Typography: modern sans-serif throughout, hierarchical sizing, restrained scale. The headline is large but not oversized — confident, not shouty.
- Imagery: real photography of named experts ("Jay", "Mick", "Michael") in story cards. Almost no iconography, no illustrations, no stock. Photography signals "these are real people we actually hired."
- Hero composition: left-aligned text stack, dual CTAs, then immediate inventory grid — pattern more associated with marketplaces (Airbnb, Upwork) than B2B SaaS.
- Density: airy hero, dense role-card grid below, generous spacing between sections.

## Copy & Messaging

- Hero headline: "Shape the frontier of AI"
- Subhead: "Mercor is organizing human intelligence to power the AI economy."
- Primary CTAs: "Start working" (to job exploration), "Learn more" (to experts page)
- Value-prop bullets (implied by role-card grid): high hourly rates ($60–$200/hr), specialized roles, rapid hiring ("hired recently"), diverse expertise required.
- Tone: forward-looking, aspirational, slightly mission-coded. Positions candidates as "shaping the frontier" rather than commodity labor — the dignity framing is the differentiator.

## Takeaways for Hire-Bee

- Show live job inventory immediately on the seeker-facing landing. Mercor's roles-grid-under-hero is far more compelling than a feature list — Hire-Bee already has job listings in its system, so surfacing 6–12 fresh roles on the public landing converts curiosity into signups without extra clicks.
- Use aspirational, identity-led copy for seekers ("Shape the frontier of AI" -> Hire-Bee could try "Get hired for what you can do, not what you wrote") while keeping recruiter copy operational/ROI-led. Two audiences, two voices — same brand.
- Real photography over illustrations for testimonials/expert stories. When Hire-Bee has shipped placements, swap any stock or illustrated humans for actual named users — Mercor proves this is a credibility multiplier that requires no design system change.
- Mercor runs almost entirely on a light palette with accents. Hire-Bee's #2a2354 can play the same role Mercor's role-card colors play — a confident accent against an otherwise quiet page — instead of dominating every surface. Inter pairs well with this restraint.
