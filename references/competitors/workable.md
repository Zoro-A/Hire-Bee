# Workable

- **Category:** ATS
- **Scraped:** 2026-05-12

## UX/UI Patterns

- Dual top-CTAs are persistent and identical to the hero buttons: "Request a demo" + "Start a free trial". Self-serve and sales-led funnels live side-by-side with no hierarchy bias.
- Card-based module rhythm: every product surface (Sourcing, ATS, HRIS, Time & Attendance, AI, Enterprise) is its own scannable card section. Lets a single landing page double as a full product directory.
- Alternating image/text feature blocks down the page, each anchored by a product screenshot or workflow diagram — keeps long-form scroll digestible.
- Pre-footer CTA panel reuses the same dual CTAs in a contrasting gradient band — captures users who scrolled past without converting.
- Social proof zone leans on a single bold stat ("30,000+ companies trusted") rather than a dense logo wall — works well when logo permissions are limited.
- Layout order: Hero -> Sourcing -> ATS -> HRIS -> Time & Attendance -> AI -> Enterprise -> Social proof / testimonials -> Pre-footer CTA -> Footer.
- Each card module follows the same micro-pattern: section eyebrow, single-line headline, 2-3 line benefit copy, contextual screenshot, inline link to learn more. Predictability speeds scanning.
- Enterprise-specific tile near the bottom for the up-market segment without dominating the main funnel — segmentation without splitting the page.
- AI is treated as its own product module rather than scattered across feature copy — capitalizes on current buyer interest.

## Visual Design

- Color palette: light greens (Workable's primary green is roughly #1ED760-adjacent), white surfaces, soft pastel gradients; subtle red/light-green wash in the pre-footer CTA band. Fresh and approachable rather than enterprise-austere.
- Typography: clean modern sans-serif with clear hierarchical heading scale; medium-weight subheads. Tight line-height in headlines, looser in body.
- Spacing/density: roomy, card-driven layout with consistent padding; less dense than Lever but covers more product surfaces per scroll than Greenhouse.
- Hero composition: large product/dashboard mockup adjacent to the headline stack and dual CTA buttons. Product-forward, not lifestyle-forward.
- Imagery: predominantly product screenshots, workflow diagrams, and data visualizations. Almost no stock photography — credibility is delivered through UI artifacts rather than emotion.
- Buttons: filled green primary, outlined secondary; both buttons sit at equal visual weight in the hero, reinforcing the "demo OR trial, your choice" message.
- Gradients are used sparingly and only in transition bands (top of feature sections, pre-footer CTA) — keeps the bulk of the page calm.

## Copy & Messaging

- Hero headline: "The future-ready HR platform"
- Subhead: "Redefining HR with intelligent tools to streamline hiring, employee data management, time tracking and payroll."
- Primary CTA: "Request a demo" (secondary: "Start a free trial")
- Value-prop bullets: all-in-one platform positioning, 30,000+ companies trusted, AI-powered automation, enterprise-grade security.
- Tone: Professional, solution-focused, confidence-driven. Emphasizes capability and breadth ("future-ready", "all-in-one") over urgency or empathy. More category-claim than pain-point.
- Voice signature: declarative noun phrases ("future-ready HR platform") rather than verb-driven outcome lines. Reads more like a brochure than a conversation — appropriate for a HR/finance audience.

## Takeaways for Hire-Bee

- The "free trial alongside demo" pattern is gold for Hire-Bee's mixed audience: keep self-serve seeker signup prominent while also offering recruiter teams a demo path. Replace the current role-only signup with twin CTAs whose secondary state reveals "I'm hiring" vs "I'm looking" framing — keeps the hero compact.
- Workable's product-screenshot-forward hero is more credible for a working ATS than illustration-heavy alternatives. Hire-Bee's recruiter dashboard is the most marketable artifact in the product — anchor the landing page hero with a real screenshot beside the headline, on the #2a2354 ground for contrast and depth.
- Adopt card-based module rhythm for marketing each Hire-Bee surface (seeker dashboard, recruiter dashboard, applications, AI matching). Cards on a dark-purple background with soft gradient highlights would feel native to the existing palette and let each surface get its own scroll section without bloating the nav.
- Add a pre-footer CTA band reusing the hero CTAs — high-leverage conversion pattern that costs almost nothing to implement and recovers scroll-past users.
- Replace dense logo walls with a single hero stat (e.g., "Trusted by N candidates and M recruiters") as soon as the numbers justify it — lower bar than collecting logos and matches Hire-Bee's earlier-stage maturity.
- Treat AI as its own named module on the marketing page rather than threading it through every feature description — current buyers shop for "AI matching" specifically and reward a dedicated section.
