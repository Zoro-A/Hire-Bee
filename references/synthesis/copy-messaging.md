# Copy & Messaging Synthesis

Compiled from 10 competitor reviews: LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, Lever, Greenhouse,
Workable, Metaview, Paradox, Eightfold, Mercor. Scraped 2026-05-12.

---

## Hero headline archetypes

### Pattern 1 — Outcome-led (imperative verb + result)

Direct command paired with a concrete outcome. Treats the visitor as the subject who
receives the benefit immediately.

- **LinkedIn**: "Hire the people you need"
- **Indeed**: "Your next job starts here"
- **Lever**: "Hiring That Doesn't Hijack Your Week"
- **Eightfold**: "How do you run 1 million interviews in 1 hour?" (question variant)

**Signature moves:** short sentence, imperative or possessive second-person, no adjectives,
no feature names. The verb carries all the weight.

### Pattern 2 — Question-led (curiosity / provocation)

Opens with an unsolved problem or a provocative scale claim. Forces the visitor to mentally
engage before the subhead explains the mechanism.

- **Eightfold**: "How do you run 1 million interviews in 1 hour?"
- **Paradox**: "Meet the AI assistant for all things hiring." (mild question / introduction)

**Signature moves:** numeric scale that sounds impossible, subhead resolves the tension,
single forward-arrow CTA follows.

### Pattern 3 — Empathy-led (recruiter pain + relief)

Names the pain the recruiter feels before naming the product. Positions the brand as
understanding the human experience of the job, not just the workflow.

- **Lever**: "Hiring That Doesn't Hijack Your Week" / "stop living in your inbox and start
  living your life"
- **Paradox**: "Hiring takes time. But our AI assistant Olivia gives you more of it."
- **Glassdoor**: "Find a job. Find your fit."

**Signature moves:** relatable pain word ("hijack", "living in your inbox"), then the
emotional payoff. Short declarative sentences. Second person throughout.

### Pattern 4 — Marketplace / identity-led

Positions the user as an agent doing meaningful work, not a person in search of a job.
Dignity-framing elevates the commodity act of job-seeking into a mission.

- **Mercor**: "Shape the frontier of AI"
- **Greenhouse**: "The _only_ hiring platform you'll ever need" (category ownership variant)
- **Workable**: "The future-ready HR platform" (aspiration/category)
- **Metaview**: "Agentic Recruiting Platform" (category label, confidence-led)

**Signature moves:** "the" + definitive noun for category ownership; or aspirational
identity word ("frontier", "future-ready") that makes the user feel they are doing
something significant.

## Subhead patterns

Subheads follow one of three structures across the 10 sites:

1. **Mechanism explainer:** "AI agents for getting the right people in seat." (Metaview) —
   resolves the curiosity gap opened by a provocative headline.

2. **Pain + payoff compound:** "Hiring takes time. But our AI assistant Olivia gives you
   more of it — automating tasks so you spend more time with people, not software." (Paradox)
   — names the problem in sentence 1; subverts it in sentence 2. The "but" pivot is
   intentional.

3. **Feature-stacked benefit list:** "Save more, hire with confidence and move faster with
   AI-powered tools that streamline every step of the hiring process – from sourcing to
   onboarding." (Greenhouse) — covers scope and benefit in one sentence; appropriate when
   the headline already landed the emotional hook.

**What to avoid:** Workable's "Redefining HR with intelligent tools to streamline hiring,
employee data management, time tracking and payroll" is the only subhead in the set that
lists feature nouns before naming a benefit — it reads like a brochure, not a conversation.

## CTA verbs

| Site | Primary CTA | Secondary CTA | Verb character |
|---|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | "Contact sales" | "Post a job" | Action-task; audience-specific |
| Indeed | "Get Started" | — | Generic onboarding; low friction |
| Glassdoor | "Search Jobs" | "Get a free salary estimate" | Task-specific; activation hook |
| Lever | "Get a Demo" | "See Pricing" | Intent-qualified; demo is commitment |
| Greenhouse | "Explore platform" | "Request a demo" | Exploration before commitment |
| Workable | "Request a demo" | "Start a free trial" | Self-serve and sales equal weight |
| Metaview | "Start for free" | "Book a demo" | Self-serve first; zero friction |
| Paradox | "Request demo" | — | Enterprise-gated; single path |
| Eightfold | "Try AI Interviewer now →" | — | Product-specific; forward arrow signals momentum |
| Mercor | "Start working" | "Learn more" | Marketplace; seeker-first |

**Verb taxonomy:**
- "Get" — easiest ask; transactional (Get Started, Get a Demo)
- "Start" — implies progress and forward motion (Start free, Start working)
- "Request" — signals qualification; appropriate for enterprise
- "Explore" — lowest commitment; invite to browse
- "Try" — product-specific; implies reversibility
- "Contact" — sales-led; appropriate only when purchase is genuinely high-touch
- "Search" — task-first; works when inventory is the product

**Pattern:** "Free" in CTA text consistently signals self-serve. The absence of "free"
signals demo-gate or enterprise gate. "Now" and a forward arrow (Eightfold) add urgency
without copy inflation.

## Tone spectrum

Positioned from utility/professional on the left to playful/empathetic on the right:

```
Utility / Professional ←————————————————————→ Playful / Empathetic

[Indeed]   [LinkedIn]   [Eightfold]   [Workable]   [Metaview]   [Greenhouse]   [Lever]   [Paradox]   [Glassdoor]   [Mercor]
```

- **Indeed**: Public utility. Short sentences. Second-person. Reads like a government
  service. Confidence through simplicity.
- **LinkedIn**: Authoritative, outcome-focused, data-backed. Imperative verbs in headlines;
  numerical proof in copy. Avoids hype.
- **Eightfold**: Enterprise-confident, forward-thinking. Repeated "humans and agents,
  united" motif. Defuses automation anxiety inline.
- **Workable**: Professional, solution-focused. Capability and breadth emphasized.
  "Future-ready" aspiration; brochure-adjacent.
- **Metaview**: Confident, action-oriented. "Get the brief" — implies the AI is a smart
  colleague. Category-staking ("Agentic").
- **Greenhouse**: Assertive, ROI-focused. Definitive language ("the only"). Uses italic
  for emphasis. Avoids hedging adjectives.
- **Lever**: Conversational, empathetic. Relatable pain language. Treats the recruiter as
  the protagonist. Short declarative sentences.
- **Paradox**: Conversational yet professional. AI-as-teammate framing. "More time with
  people, not software" — human-centric payoff.
- **Glassdoor**: Encouraging, slightly playful. "Find your fit" emotional outcome. Uses
  "you love" in copy. Friend-with-data positioning.
- **Mercor**: Aspirational, mission-coded. Dignity-framing for seekers. "Shape the
  frontier" elevates job-seeking into identity. Most emotionally ambitious in the set.

## Value-prop framing

Three dominant frameworks observed:

### Stats-led
Lead with a specific number that proves scale or performance. Number does the persuasion;
prose explains the mechanism.
- LinkedIn: "24% less likely to reopen a role within 12 months"
- Eightfold: "1.6B+ career trajectories, 1.6M+ skills indexed, 50+ bias-mitigation variables"
- Workable: "30,000+ companies trusted"
- Lever: "Faster Time to Hire, Faster Interview Scheduling, More Qualified Applicants" (KPI strip)

### Benefit-led
Names the outcome the user feels, not the feature that delivers it. Works best for products
with complex or novel mechanics that resist brief explanation.
- Lever subhead: "stop living in your inbox and start living your life"
- Paradox subhead: "automating tasks so you spend more time with people, not software"
- Greenhouse: "Save more, hire with confidence and move faster"

### Identity-led
Positions the user as a person doing meaningful, forward-looking work — not just a person
using software.
- Mercor: "Shape the frontier of AI" / "organizing human intelligence"
- Metaview: "built for winning talent"
- Eightfold: "Your team makes the final decisions" (restores human agency inside the AI narrative)

**Timing rule:** Stats-led works best once real numbers exist. Benefit-led works from day
one. Identity-led works best when your audience has a strong professional self-image
(engineers, senior TA leaders) and wants to see that reflected.

## Gap analysis vs current Hire-Bee

Reference file: `frontend/src/pages/LandingPage.jsx`

**Current Hire-Bee hero copy (verbatim from `LandingPage.jsx`):**

```jsx
// Badge / eyebrow:
"Production-ready hiring platform"

// H1:
"Run hiring from first resume to final interview."

// Subhead:
"Complete flows for Job Seekers, Recruiters, and Admin with direct FastAPI integration."

// CTAs:
"Continue as Job Seeker"
"Continue as Recruiter"
"Continue as Admin"

// Feature list (right card):
"Auth and RBAC"
"Resume parsing and matching"
"CV + cover letter generation"
"Applications and status tracking"
"Interview scheduling and email logs"
```

**Diagnosis:**
- The eyebrow ("Production-ready") is a dev-team status note, not a market-facing category claim.
- The H1 describes a workflow range, not an outcome or a promise. "First resume to final interview" is a scope statement; it tells the visitor what the product covers, not why they should care.
- The subhead names user roles ("Job Seekers, Recruiters, and Admin") and a technical stack ("direct FastAPI integration"). Neither detail speaks to a visitor's goal. "FastAPI integration" is invisible to non-technical buyers.
- CTA verbs are role-labels, not action verbs. "Continue as" is a confirmation UI pattern (post-login), not a marketing CTA.
- The feature list uses internal/technical labels: "Auth and RBAC", "Interview scheduling and email logs" — jargon that recruiter and seeker audiences cannot map to their own goals.

**Specific copy upgrades (8):**

1. **Reframe the eyebrow as a category claim.** Change "Production-ready hiring platform"
   to "AI-Powered Hiring, for both sides." Signals category (AI), signals two-sided
   marketplace, removes internal jargon. (Inspired by: Metaview's "Agentic Recruiting
   Platform" eyebrow.)

2. **Rewrite the H1 as an empathy-led outcome.** The current scope statement can become a
   pain-relief line. Candidate: "Stop screening resumes. Start hiring people." — short,
   imperative, names the pain, implies the mechanism. Alternatively, the Lever pattern:
   "Hiring That Works for Both Sides." (Inspired by: Lever's "Doesn't Hijack Your Week"
   and Paradox's "gives you more of it.")

3. **Rewrite the subhead as a mechanism explainer.** Replace "Complete flows for Job
   Seekers, Recruiters, and Admin with direct FastAPI integration." with: "Hire-Bee's AI
   parses resumes, generates CVs, and schedules interviews — so seekers get found and
   recruiters get time back." Names the mechanism (AI), names what it does (three verbs),
   delivers dual-audience payoff in one sentence. (Inspired by: Greenhouse's "Save more,
   hire with confidence and move faster" structure.)

4. **Replace role-label CTAs with action verbs.** Change "Continue as Job Seeker" →
   "Find your next role" and "Continue as Recruiter" → "Start hiring smarter." Role
   selection can remain on the register page; the CTA text should name what the user
   gets, not who they are. (Inspired by: Mercor's "Start working" and Glassdoor's
   "Find your fit" emotional framing.)

5. **Replace the feature-label list with benefit-led verb phrases.** The right-hand card
   lists "Auth and RBAC" and "Interview scheduling and email logs." Rewrite these as
   verb-first benefit statements that a seeker or recruiter can immediately relate to:
   - "Auth and RBAC" → "Secure sign-in for seekers and recruiters"
   - "Resume parsing and matching" → "AI reads every resume so you don't have to"
   - "CV + cover letter generation" → "Generate a tailored CV in under a minute"
   - "Applications and status tracking" → "Track every application in one place"
   - "Interview scheduling and email logs" → "Book interviews without the back-and-forth"
   (Inspired by: Metaview's verb-first agent cards: "Finds and outreaches…", "Reviews
   every inbound…".)

6. **Add a provocative question variant for the H1 (A/B candidate).** Test Eightfold's
   question-led archetype: "How do you screen 500 resumes before your morning standup?"
   — frames the AI matching as scale relief. Resolves in the subhead. (Inspired by:
   Eightfold's "How do you run 1 million interviews in 1 hour?" curiosity-gap pattern.)

7. **Add seeker identity copy to differentiate the two cards.** Currently both audiences
   read the same hero. Mercor uses "Shape the frontier of AI" for seekers — aspirational,
   dignity-forward. Hire-Bee can try: "Get hired for what you can actually do." for the
   seeker card — frames the AI matching as breaking the resume-bias problem, which is a
   real seeker pain point and a genuine differentiator.

8. **Replace "Continue as Admin" CTA with a trust micro-copy line.** The Admin CTA in
   `LandingPage.jsx` is an internal link that signals unfinished product to marketing
   visitors. Remove it from the public landing and replace the space with a one-line trust
   statement: "Trusted by hiring teams at [X companies] — or — "No credit card required."
   (Inspired by: LinkedIn's "24% less likely to reopen a role" one-line stat that does
   persuasion work without a full testimonial block.)
